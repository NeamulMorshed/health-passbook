# Omra — Deep UX & Product Design Audit
**Date:** 2026-06-01 · **Version audited:** v2.12.0+41 · **Method:** Heuristic eval (Nielsen 10) + persona journey walk + design-emotion review + ui-ux-pro-max design intelligence
**Scope:** 48 screens / ~28.5k LOC (`vitalpath_flutter`), 3 portals (Patient / Doctor / Family Member)

> **Note vs. old audit.** The original `AUDIT_REPORT.md` scored the app **5.1/10**. Nearly all of its findings (security S-01→S-08, UX-1→UX-18, accessibility 9a–9c, T-01→T-04) are now **closed** per `BACKLOG.md`. This is a **fresh audit of the current state** — it does not rehash fixed items. The app is materially stronger now; remaining issues are subtler: emotional flatness, generic visual language, residual flow gaps, and self-violated design rules.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Omra is now a **functionally complete, well-architected** chronic-care app. Routing guards, defensive streams, freshness timestamps, empty states, needs-attention surfacing, and accessibility contrast fixes are all in place. The skeleton is healthy.

What's missing is the **last 20% that separates "works" from "loved"**:

1. **Emotionally flat.** A daily diabetes/hypertension companion should feel calm, warm, and reassuring. Current UI is competent but generic — flat white cards, single green, hard 0ms state changes, almost no motion or haptics. It reads "internal tool," not "trusted health companion."
2. **Generic visual system.** One brand green + gray text + white BentoCards repeated on every screen. No depth, no warmth tier, no celebratory moments. The AI-insights card (cyan→indigo gradient) is the *only* place with visual energy — and ui-ux-pro-max flags that exact gradient as an anti-pattern for healthcare.
3. **Self-violated design rules.** The team's own `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` says *never raw hex* and *never Material Icons where HugeIcons exists*. Reality: **44 raw `Color(0xFF…)` in screens** and **194 Material `Icons.` usages**. Consistency is drifting.
4. **Residual flow gaps.** A few one-way / dead-end moments remain (detailed §4).

**Fresh score: 7.4 / 10** (up from 5.1). Ceiling is ~9 with the polish + emotion work below.

## 3. Design Emotion Assessment

**Target emotion for a daily chronic-care app:** *calm, reassured, gently encouraged, in-control* — never anxious, never clinical-cold, never gamified-childish.

**Current emotional read: neutral-clinical.** It doesn't cause anxiety (good) but it also never *reassures* or *delights*. Specific gaps:

- **No "you're OK" moment.** Karim's #1 need is a 2-second "Is she OK?" answer. The home has the data but no single hero reassurance state ("✅ Aisha is on track today") with calm green warmth. Aisha herself never gets a "you've done everything today — nicely done" close-of-day moment.
- **Flat surfaces, no depth.** Every card is white + 0.5px border. No elevation language, no soft shadows (only 6 `BoxShadow` in whole app), no warm-tint backgrounds for positive states. Everything has equal visual weight → nothing feels special.
- **Instant, mechanical state changes.** 1 `HapticFeedback` call and 3 animations across 48 screens. Marking a dose taken, completing a streak, accepting an invite — all snap instantly. No satisfying microfeedback. ui-ux-pro-max: micro-interactions should be 150–300ms.
- **The one burst of color is the wrong one.** The cyan→indigo AI gradient (`home_screen.dart:318`) is the only vivid element — and ui-ux-pro-max explicitly lists *"AI purple/pink gradients"* and *"bright neon"* as **healthcare anti-patterns**. It pulls the eye to the lowest-value card (AI insights, ranked #12 for Aisha).
- **Celebration is text-only.** Streaks/HP shown as `✅` emoji + number (`gamification_screen.dart:338`) — and emoji-as-icon violates the team's own no-emoji rule. No confetti, no ring-fill animation, no warmth.

**ui-ux-pro-max emotional direction (validated):** style = *Accessible & Ethical* (medical teal + health green, WCAG AAA, 44px targets, **reduced motion, avoid neon/AI-gradients**). Typography mood for warmth = *Soft Rounded / Accessibility-First* (Atkinson Hyperlegible, Figtree, Nunito Sans). Omra's Open Sans is fine but neutral; a softer display face for headings would add warmth without losing legibility.

## 4. User Flow Gaps (current state)

Walked all three persona journeys against the live router + screens. Most P0/P1 gaps from the old audit are **closed**. Remaining:

**Patient (Aisha)**
- **G-1 · Card overload on home.** 11 stacked cards; her #1 task (meds due) sits behind Freshness + GetStarted + Awareness before Tasks. Most-urgent-without-scrolling is at risk on small devices. *(IA / heuristic #8)*
- **G-2 · No mark-taken undo.** A mis-tap on a dose has no recovery; affects trust in adherence data. *(heuristic #3/#5)*
- **G-3 · AI card mis-ranked.** Highest visual prominence on lowest-value content. *(emotion / IA)*

**Doctor (Dr. Rahman)**
- **G-4 · "Today's schedule" still absent from dashboard.** Old audit flagged it ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐; dashboard shows a *Today count* stat but no **appointment list with times**. He still taps into Appointments to see who/when. *(flow gap — partially open)*
- **G-5 · Needs-attention has no "why now / since when."** Pills show "<50% adherence" / "abnormal vitals" but no trend or timestamp; low explainability. *(heuristic #10)*

**Family Member (Karim)**
- **G-6 · No single status hero.** `_OnboardingGuidanceCard` + per-member detail exist, but the 2-second green/amber "Is she OK?" banner is not the first, dominant element. Data is present; emotional payload isn't surfaced. *(emotion / persona #1 need)*
- **G-7 · Member switching is post-frame setState** (`caregiver_home:105`) — first paint can flash empty before auto-selecting first member. Minor jank. *(polish)*

**Cross-role**
- **G-8 · Role switch = forced sign-out.** A user who is both patient and caregiver (common: a patient who also cares for a parent) cannot hold both; `user_select` mismatch dialog signs them out. Real-world friction for the exact multi-role family scenario the app targets. *(product/flow)*

## 5. Generic UI → Distinctive UI (improvement plan)

The brief: *"improve existing generic UI."* The UI is generic because it leans 100% on one primitive (white `BentoCard`) + one accent (green) + size-only hierarchy. Concrete upgrades, all within the existing token system:

1. **Introduce a depth + warmth tier.** Add 2 surface treatments to the design system: (a) `surfaceElevated` with a soft 1-2px ambient shadow for the single hero card per screen; (b) tinted positive surfaces (`successLight`/`primaryXLight` fills) for "on track" states so good news *feels* good. Reserve flat-white for secondary cards.
2. **Kill the AI gradient; re-rank the card.** Replace cyan→indigo with `primaryXLight` surface + green accent (on-brand, non-anti-pattern), and move AI insights below the fold. Frees the eye for the meds task.
3. **Add a daily "status hero" per portal.** Patient: "You've taken 2 of 3 — one more tonight." Caregiver: "✅ Aisha is on track." Doctor: today's schedule list. One emotionally-resonant card at the top, calm color-coded.
4. **Micro-interactions (150–300ms).** Animate: dose check-off (scale+fade+light haptic), streak/HP ring fill, invite-accepted success, pull-to-refresh "Updated just now" toast. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
5. **Skeleton loaders** replacing bare spinners on the 3 dashboards (ui-ux-pro-max High severity).
6. **Typographic warmth.** Keep Open Sans for body; consider a soft display face (Figtree / rounded) for headings + greeting only. Small change, big warmth delta.
7. **Pay down the consistency debt** (mechanical, high-ROI): migrate **194 Material `Icons.` → HugeIcons**, **44 raw hex → tokens**, dedupe the 3 `_greeting()` helpers into one util. This alone lifts the "crafted" feeling.
8. **Status-color system, not just dots.** Positive/caution/critical should each carry icon + tint + label consistently (legend exists — extend the *fill* treatment app-wide).

ui-ux-pro-max recommended palette to layer in (medical, non-generic, accessible): primary teal `#0891B2`, health green `#22C55E` CTA, calm bg `#F0FDFA`, deep text `#134E4A`. Omra's `#0F9D77` green is already close — the gap is **secondary/tint depth**, not hue.
